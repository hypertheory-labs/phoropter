# The indexer guide, revised from what two .NET indexers found

Seersucker (indexers are programs in `indexers/`) guessed that an agent with only
`indexers/README.md` and some existing code could write an indexer that passes
`phoropter index --check`. Jeff tested it. A fresh agent, given his Roslyn code from
glass-blower and the guide, and told never to read phoropter's source, built
`phoropter-index-roslyn`, a syntax-only indexer that passes on a real solution (397
definitions in 0.15s). Then, at Jeff's suggestion, it wrapped scip-dotnet for comparison.
It logged every question the guide didn't answer, thirty of them, in its own
`QUESTIONS.md`. That file is the evidence for this plan.

The comparison was decisive. The syntax-only indexer found 338 definitions in 0.2s, on any
commit, offline, with nothing restored. scip-dotnet found 251 in 12s with a warm cache,
had no definitions for records' positional properties, primary constructors or extension
members, collided on namespaces, and failed offline with an empty cache after two minutes
of NuGet retries. The roslyn indexer stays where it is, third-party, on `PATH`. It's the
first living proof of that half of the contract.

## Decision: The guide says what condition the checkout is in
- where: `indexers/README.md`
- quote: `` `<dir>` is the repository checked out at one commit, at a temporary path. Each ``

**Because (the experiment, #1 and #25):** the agent's first question was whether the
checkout is restored or built. It isn't, and it may be offline: phoropter checks out a
commit, perhaps years old, and runs the indexer on it. The guide says so, and says an
indexer that works from source alone is the one that keeps working on old commits. The
comparison measured why: the build-based indexer needed the network and gigabytes, and
failed without them.

## Decision: A package is named by the compiler's name for it, and may not be unique
- where: `indexers/README.md`
- quote: `` `package.json`. For Go, the module path in `go.mod`. For .NET, the project's ``

**Because (the experiment, #22 and #27):** "PackageId or AssemblyName" let the two indexers
name one package differently, and phoropter asks for a package by name. The guide says
`AssemblyName` for .NET, which is the compiler's name and scip-dotnet's, and readable
without loading anything. It also says a package name may name several projects in one
repository (copies of `ServiceDefaults` in four solutions). Then the first in path order
answers, and no symbol is ever invented across copies. Inventing them was the real bug the
comparison caught.

## Decision: Name things as your language's SCIP indexer does, except where it loses information
- where: `indexers/README.md`
- quote: `Leave out local variables. A plan anchors what a reader can find by name. Every type,`

**Because (the experiment, #3, #4, #12, #24):** following an established indexer's naming
answered constructors, indexers, operators and events. Following it blindly would have
copied its losses: scip-dotnet keeps only the innermost namespace segment, so same-named
types collide, and it doesn't encode generic arity, so `Foo` and `Foo<T>` collide. The
guide says to follow the established naming, except that full namespace paths are kept and
arity is written when a name is reused (`Foo#`, then the CLR's `` `Foo`1`# ``). It also
covers partial types (one definition, the first part in path order) and what's optional:
parameters and type parameters, better left out.

## Decision: The output's edges are said plainly
- where: `indexers/README.md`
- quote: `` - **`missing`:** packages named on the command line that you couldn't find. Phoropter ``

**Because (the experiment, #10, #26, #29):** `missing` means any package the indexer knows
of but couldn't index, whether or not it was named, so a project that failed to load
never looks absent. It may be omitted when empty. Definitions come only from files in the
checkout: generated files in `obj/` and source-generator output aren't definitions, since
no plan can quote them.

## Decision: Phoropter stops an indexer that runs too long
- where: `src/indexers.ts`
- quote: `const r = spawnSync(info.command[0], [...info.command.slice(1), dir, ...packages], { cwd: info.cwd, encoding: 'utf8', maxBuffer: 1 << 30 });`

**Because (the experiment, #30):** phoropter runs an indexer with no time limit, so a NuGet
retry loop would stall a whole build. After a limit, phoropter stops it, and its anchors
show `no-index`, saying it timed out. The guide says what an indexer can expect: a time
limit, and no promise of the network. The limit is 5 minutes (Jeff), with
`PHOROPTER_INDEX_TIMEOUT` (in seconds) to change it. The syntax-only indexer takes a
fraction of a second, a build-based one around a minute on a fresh machine, and anything
past five minutes is more likely stuck than slow. Two and ten minutes were weighed.

## Decision: `phoropter symbols` groups its output by scheme
- where: `src/cli.ts`
- quote: `const candidates = indexers().filter((i) => i.extensions.includes(ext));`

**Because (the experiment, #23):** with two indexers claiming `.cs`, their symbols came out
interleaved. Anchors were never ambiguous, since a symbol's scheme picks its indexer, but a
plan author reading the list couldn't tell them apart at a glance. Output is grouped
under a heading per scheme, and `--scheme` picks one. A per-repository preference would be
configuration, which waits for a need.

## Decline: Bringing the roslyn indexer into `indexers/dotnet/`
**Because (Claude, not asked):** it's the only indexer found on `PATH`, written outside this
repository from the guide alone. Moving it in would leave that half of the contract with
no living example. It can move in later, if keeping it outside stops being worth that.
