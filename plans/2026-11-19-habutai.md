# The guide and the pointer say what the trial's agent couldn't know

The trial's agent (`shelf/trial.md`) did well with what it was given, and three of its
slips came from what it wasn't given. It read AGENTS.md and went straight to the
extension, leaving the first draft alone. It answered notes by rewriting the draft but
kept the notes file, because the guide says only that *converting* deletes notes. And it
turned the draft's prompts into its harness's multiple-choice tool. A fourth thing is
just ours: the guide's labels say "Claude" to an agent that may not be. Here, each of
these is fixed in phoropter's own words, not by linking to loom's agreement, since a
fresh agent reads the guide and not the link.

## Decision: The pointer says to offer the first draft before other work
- where: `init.go`
- quote: `` format. The first plan, "How we work here" (the oldest in ` + "`plans/`" + `, or ``

**Because (the trial, then Claude and Jeff):** the pointer said "read both before other work",
and the agent did read, then went where the person's enthusiasm was. Nothing said the
first draft was its move. Proposed wording, replacing the pointer's last sentence:

> Read both before other work. While the first plan is still a draft, offer to work
> through it with the person before anything else.

`init` prints it, the README shows it, and a test keeps them the same. "Offer", not "the
draft comes first" (Jeff): it isn't a rules engine; two intelligences are present, and
an invitation to discussion beats a rule. It's also the template's own word, "the agent
offers, and nothing gets stamped", and the person can still say "later".

## Decision: The guide says answering notes rewrites the draft and deletes them
- where: `guide.md`
- quote: `[annotating agreement](https://github.com/JeffryGonzalez/annotations.nvim)'s format.`

**Because (the trial):** the agent rewrote the draft to answer Jeff's note and left the
notes file "for conversion to consume", so phoropter showed the note as "quote not
found". We dissolve correctly here only because loom's agreement is in our heads. The
guide gains, in its own words: notes quote the draft's lines, so while they exist the
draft doesn't change. To answer them, rewrite the draft taking in every note, then
delete the notes file, all of it or none. A reply that isn't a rewrite goes in
conversation, and the draft stays as it is.

## Decision: The guide says prompts are answered in notes or in conversation, not a form
- where: `guide.md`
- quote: `` `Select` lists its options as the first bulleted list; `Affirm` is yes or no. A draft ``

**Because (the trial, then Jeff and Claude):** the agent put the draft's prompts to Jeff
through its multiple-choice tool, one after another. The answers survived, since
conversion folds in answers from anywhere, but annotating in place, half the point of a
draft, got skipped. One sentence: the person answers in notes beside the draft, or in
conversation; don't turn a draft's prompts into a form. It's the sibling of the
template's "wondering isn't asking": both protect the person's own pace.

## Decision: The guide's labels name whoever drafted, not Claude
- where: `guide.md`
- quote: `` so: `Claude, unanswered, so its fallback`. ``

**Because (Jeff and Claude):** the trial's agent was Copilot, and the guide told it to
write "Claude, unanswered". The Sources paragraph already says "or whichever agent"; the
fallback label and the conversion paragraph now say the same: the drafter's own name,
`Copilot, unanswered, so its fallback`. Not "the agent": Jeff is named in reasons, so
the agent is too, the way the pair talks. What a name is for, and the model in metadata,
are on the shelf (`shelf/identity.md`).

## Decline: Noticing a stale pointer in repositories that pasted the old one
**Because (Claude, not asked):** every repository that ran `init` before this has the
old pointer pasted in, and muslin chose pasting over something phoropter keeps current.
`doctor` could compare them. But there's one such repository, the trial's, and Jeff can
paste the new line by hand. When there are more, it's worth a plan.
