# As built: burlap

Phoropter's first revisit. Written straight to `plans/` without a draft in a cart,
because how to do it was already agreed in sorrel's cart. The revisit's outcome:
quince's ground holds except for the one decline, which is reversed. Nothing departed
from this plan, so there's no `Changed:`.

One finding, worth having before an "open guesses" page exists: "no verdict" isn't
"open". Before this as-built, five guesses had no verdict, and only burlap's own was
really open (it's answered below). Gimlet's two and tamarind's one were answered in `Changed:` entries, before grommet
gave answers a kind. Pumice's `@source` guess was answered by vellum, a different plan,
two hops away through vellum's guess. A page of open guesses would have to say which it
means, or it would list four answered guesses as open.

## Chose: On a guess, the verdict sits in the sentence that links its answer
- where: `src/render.ts`
- quote: ``return `<p class="pair">Answered in ${entry.answeredBy.map((a) => `${link('answered-by', 'answeredBy', a, 'the as-built')}: ${verdictHtml(a.entry)}`).join(', ')}.</p>`;``

**Because (Claude, not asked):** "Answered in the as-built: held." The verdict belongs to
the answer, so on the guess it travels with the link to it rather than becoming a
property of the guess itself. An agent finds it as `verdict` inside the guess's entry
either way. Disproved and partly disproved are colored like a missing quote; held and
not tested aren't colored at all.

## Chose: An answer with no verdict still shows its link, with a note
- where: `src/render.ts`
- quote: `: '<span class="note">no verdict (it doesn’t open with held, disproved, partly disproved, or not tested)</span>';`

**Because (Claude, not asked):** the plan said it says so plainly. The note names the
four words, so a writer who sees it knows what to change in the next as-built.

## Answered: An answer's first word is the verdict its author meant
**Answer (measured):** held, on eight of eight. Every answer in mock-control and
phoropter opens with a verdict (seven held, one disproved), and none of their prose
contradicts it in a way that would mislead. Quince's "held on what exists, and not
yet tested against what would disprove it" reads as held, which is what its author (Claude) meant: the guess
held on every case that existed. That's one ambiguous case resolved the author's way,
not a test of the rule against an answer written to mislead it.
