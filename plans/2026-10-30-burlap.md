# Revisit quince: an answer's verdict is structure

Quince read grommet's kinds and paired each `Answered:` with its `Guess:`, and it
declined to pull the verdict out of the answer. Jeff asked for exactly that in sorrel's
cart, so this plan stands where quince stood and asks its question again: how much of
grommet's answer format should the page carry as structure?

## Decision: What moved is our understanding, not the world or the code
- where: `plans/2026-10-30-quince.md › Reading the verdict (held, disproved, not tested) out of an answer's prose`

**Because (Jeff and Claude):** grommet already said an answer opens with held,
disproved, partly disproved, or not tested when quince declined it. Quince read that
as prose and called pulling a word out of it a guess. It's a convention with a closed
set of four, which is structure written in words, like the `Because (…)` that marl now
reads. What we learned was what it's for. "Which guesses are still open, and which were
disproved?" is the question an agent arriving at code most needs answered, and today it
takes two hops per guess (Jeff).

## Decision: The verdict is the answer's first word or words, when they're one of grommet's four
- where: `src/plan.ts`
- quote: `entry.source = /^\*\*(?:Because|Answer) \((.*?)\):\*\*/.exec(entry.body)?.[1];`

**Because (grommet, Jeff and Claude):** after `**Answer (…):**`, the text starts with
`held`, `disproved`, `partly disproved`, or `not tested`, followed by punctuation, a
space, or the end. Anything after it ("as far as a dry run can tell," "on ten of
ten") stays prose, where the reader sees it. Every answer in mock-control and phoropter
opens this way today: six `held` and one `disproved`.

## Decision: The verdict shows on the answer and on its guess
- where: `src/render.ts`
- quote: ``if (entry.answers) return `<p class="pair">Answers ${link('answers', 'answers', entry.answers, 'the plan’s guess')}.</p>`;``

**Because (Jeff):** on the guess is where it's needed. A reader on a plan page sees
"Answered in the as-built: held" without leaving the page, and one pass over the plan
pages finds every guess that was disproved. Both carry `itemprop="verdict"`. A guess
with no answer has no verdict, which is how a reader finds the open ones.

## Decision: An answer that doesn't open with one of the four says it has no verdict
**Because (Claude, agreed by Jeff):** the same as an answer with no matching guess. The
page says so plainly rather than guessing at the prose.

## Decline: Reading qualifiers, or a fifth verdict
**Because (Claude):** "held on what exists, and not yet tested against what would
disprove it" is quince's own answer, and its first word is `held`. Reading on to decide
it's really "not tested" would be the guess quince declined, and it would overrule the
answer's author. The prose is right under it.

## Guess: An answer's first word is the verdict its author meant
**Disproved if:** an answer opens with a verdict that its own prose contradicts, so that
a reader trusting the structure would be misled. Quince's answer is the case to look
at: "held," then "not yet tested."
