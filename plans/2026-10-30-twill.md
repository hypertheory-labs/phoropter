# Phoropter owns the plan format, and every site serves its guide

The plan format was decided across four plans in aide, the repository where the
thinking happened: lanyard (the format), grommet (as-builts), bodkin (revisits), and awl
(when an as-built freezes). Aide is ephemeral, and what it decided is meant to live
here and in projects like it. So phoropter takes the format over, and states it as a
guide in the present tense, which every generated site serves next to its profile.

## Decision: Phoropter owns the plan format, and the guide is how it's stated
- where: `CLAUDE.md`
- quote: `changing how phoropter reads plans; a format question goes to aide, not into code.`

**Because (Jeff):** "aide is ephemeral … you own it all." A guide that documents a
format someone else decides goes stale, so the guide and the format are one thing. From
here, a change to the format is a phoropter plan, and the guide changes in the same
work. Aide's four format plans stay where they are, as history. The guide names them
in plain text, since nobody needs to click into a frozen past. Aide records the handoff
on its side (Jeff).

## Decision: The guide is markdown in the repository, `guide.md`, rendered into every site at `guide/`
- where (new): `guide.md`

**Because (Jeff and Claude):** it's a few pages, and changes to it should read as diffs,
which strings inside `render.ts` don't. It's global like the profile: it describes the
tool, not the repository being generated. The page says which phoropter version it
describes (the tool's commit when it runs from a checkout, otherwise its package
version), the same way every page says its commit. The guide also says that older plans
may use earlier forms and are read as written.

## Decision: The home page's orientation and every header link the guide
- where: `src/render.ts`
- quote: `<a class="profile-link" href="${rel(here, 'profile/')}">profile</a>`

**Because (Jeff):** someone who has never heard of loom should be able to find out what
a plan is from any page. Beside the profile link, since the two answer neighboring
questions: what a plan is, and what the page's structure means.

## Decision: CLAUDE.md points at the guide for the format
- where: `CLAUDE.md`
- quote: `- **Plans:** `

**Because (Jeff and Claude):** one statement of the rules. CLAUDE.md keeps what's
specific to working in this repository (carts, drafts by hand, the test data) and sends
the format to `guide.md`.

## Decision: An as-built freezes once someone else could have read it
- where: `` plans/2026-10-30-tamarind.as-built.md › The branch started at `9b1f311`, not the plan's base ``

**Because (Jeff, then aide's Claude and Claude):** the format shouldn't prescribe a git
workflow, or make a team say "we'd love to, but our workflow doesn't fit" (Jeff). Awl
said committed (merged, for branch work), and "pushed" was this draft's first answer,
but a push means different things on a fork, a pull-request branch, or a shared remote.
What the rule protects is someone who anchored to an entry, and that can only happen
once someone else could have read it; when that is depends on how a team shares work.
The guide says so, with "merged into your main line" for anyone unsure. Measured here:
burlap's as-built was corrected 18 seconds after it was written, on its branch;
tamarind's, from the round that trimmed symbol views to the evidence, eleven minutes
after, ten seconds after it was merged to main and before Jeff pushed. Awl was written
because of tamarind's. Neither could have been read in between, so neither is a breach.
Phoropter can't see any of this and doesn't need to, since it never flags.

## Decision: The guide says what squash merges and rebases do to `head:`
- where (new): `guide.md`

**Because (Jeff and aide's Claude):** plans record commits, and a squash or rebase
replaces the commits an as-built's `head:` names, after the as-built was written. Better
a team reads that in the guide than finds it out. Today, when `head:` doesn't resolve,
phoropter shows it as text and drops the head view, which the guide states as current
behavior. Jeff thinks nudging teams toward meaningful commits that aren't squashed may
be the right direction anyway, so the guide says that too, as advice.

## Decision: Verdicts are read case-insensitively
- where: `src/plan.ts`
- quote: `entry.verdict = VERDICTS.find((v) => after.startsWith(v) && !/[a-z]/i.test(after.charAt(v.length)));`

**Because (Claude, not asked):** the guide says lowercase, but "Held." is the same
verdict. A rule that fails on a capital letter is a rule nobody can see working.

## Decline: Resolving the guide's references, and a self-check for stale rules
**Because (Jeff and Claude):** the guide cites the plans behind each rule, and phoropter
could show when one has since been revisited. But the guide is served inside every
repository's site, where phoropter's plans are another repository's. Resolving them
needs either reading two repositories or a citation form the guide alone uses. That's
its own plan. For now the citations are plain text.

## Decline: Reconciling a squashed `head:` with the commit that replaced it
**Because (Claude, not asked):** it would mean guessing which commit on the main line
holds the work, from messages or trees, and it hasn't happened in a real repository yet.
When it does, that's a plan with a case to measure.

## Decline: Revisiting awl through `revisits:`
**Because (Claude, not asked):** `revisits:` names a plan in the same repository, and
awl is in aide. Since phoropter now owns the format, the freezing rule changes in a
phoropter plan, this one, which names awl in its reason.

## Guess: Someone who has never heard of loom can write a plan and an as-built from the guide alone
**Disproved if:** the first person or agent outside this conversation to try needs
something the guide doesn't say, or does something it says not to because it was
unclear.
